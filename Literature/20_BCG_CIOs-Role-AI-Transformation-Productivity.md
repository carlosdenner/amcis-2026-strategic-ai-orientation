# The CIO's Role in AI Value Creation (BCG, 2025)

Source: https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/cios-role-in-ai-transformation-and-productivity
Authors: Jean-Francois Bobier, Abhik Chatterjee, Ruth Ebeling, Michael Grebe, Dmitry Makarov, Jonathan Milde, Rohit Nalgirkar, Juuso Soininen

## Key Takeaways
- GenAI can address up to 50% of tech function costs and deliver up to 30% gains in efficiency. Programming assistants, service desk tools, and knowledge management are particularly compelling use cases.
- Beyond adopting GenAI in their own domain, CIOs can help accelerate a broader AI transformation by taking point on three key challenges: complying with emerging regulations, fostering scaling, and avoiding shadow IT.
- To succeed in this effort, CIOs should lead on responsible AI, establish a modular and scalable architecture that supports reusability, and develop governance practices that help companies reap the benefits of experimentation without creating a zoo of tools.

## Optimizing IT Productivity

Across industries, generative AI (GenAI) has generated excitement. But GenAI is still in its early stages, and relatively few organizations thus far have managed to unlock its potential. Research conducted for BCG's report "Where's the Value in AI?" reveals that just 26% of companies are creating real value from AI. Many more are struggling to move beyond the proof of concept (POC) stage. And more than 50% say that legacy IT architectures are hindering by limiting scaling, reusability, and data availability.

For CIOs, GenAI is exerting pressure to deliver concrete benefits from the technology. But it is also creating two opportunities:

1. GenAI has significant implications for the tech function, helping CIOs optimize their company's IT productivity. About a third of the value potential of scaling AI lies in enabling functions such as customer service, procurement, and IT.

2. GenAI offers CIOs an opportunity to expand their circle of influence within the organization. Companies need to tackle and communicate risks, build a foundation for scaling, and create clear policies governing AI tools, uses, and regulatory compliance.

### Paths to Value

Three types of GenAI use cases are particularly compelling:
- Programming assistants (e.g., GitHub Copilot) - 30%+ productivity boost
- IT service desk tools - automate ticket resolution and incident management
- Knowledge management - natural language interfaces with RAG systems

### What the CIO Should Do

1. **Focus on value** - Prioritize use cases on the basis of value, looking at addressable costs and expected efficiency gains.
2. **Rethink processes** - Zero-based approach to reimagine processes from scratch before automating with GenAI.
3. **Adopt new vendor negotiation strategies** - Align payments with performance or outcomes for fixed-price contracts.
4. **Invest in change management** - The 10/20/70 rule: 10% data science, 20% tech stack, 70% processes/people/change management.
5. **Have the right talent in place** - Build internal talent with hybrid approach (internal teams + selected vendors).

## Positioning IT as an Innovation Partner of the Business

BCG research: At 86% of AI future-built companies, IT leads or co-leads with the business on GenAI (vs 54% for AI stagnating companies).

### Three Key Challenges

1. **Complying with Emerging Regulations** - EU AI Act, Asia-Pacific regulations, US state legislation. Companies need to determine which regulations apply, ensure compliance, and confirm proper service to stakeholders.

2. **Ensuring Scalability, Reusability, and Data Availability** - Technical debt, difficulty accessing data, incompatible platforms, and costly isolated development hinder scaling. Without proper application and data architecture, AI value remains out of reach.

3. **Avoiding New GenAI-Related Shadow IT** - Unsanctioned use of GenAI tools exposes organizations to data privacy, security, and compliance risks, and leads to uncoordinated tech investments.

### What the CIO Should Do

1. **Lead on Responsible AI (RAI)** - 74% of AI future-built companies continuously monitor compliance with their RAI framework. Work with cross-functional teams including legal, HR, and business units.

2. **Establish a modular, scalable architecture** - Move from patchwork of silos to enterprise AI foundation with four components: foundation models, GenAI platforms, data layer, and self-monitoring capability. Use archetypes (8 archetypical AI applications) as blueprints for efficient deployment.

3. **Develop governance that keeps shadow IT at bay** - Establish clear governance framework, consolidate tools, channel innovative efforts productively and safely within controlled environments.

## Conclusion
CIOs who lead the charge on GenAI will capture value within the tech function, enable the organization to do the same, and help steer and shape its future.
